Project 4 — Preference Elicitation

A movie recommender that learns your taste from a handful of choices, and a user study comparing two ways of asking. The system estimates a preference vector w over {{ n_features }} interpretable movie features using a Plackett–Luce model; you stay in control of every input, and you can override the result at the end.

The two designs being compared

Design 1 — you repeatedly choose which of two films you would rather watch.

Design 2 — you are shown ten films and rank them, by picking your favourite of those remaining, over and over.

Every participant does both, in counterbalanced order.

What is under the hood

{{ n_movies }} films from the IMDB 5000 dataset, {{ n_pool }} of them well-known enough to show to participants.

Preferences are estimated by MAP under a Plackett–Luce likelihood — the same estimator for both designs, so the comparison is not confounded.

The report covers the features (Task 1), the ranking model (Task 2) and the full study protocol (Task 3).

Curious about the extension? The adaptive question-selection demo shows how much faster the model learns when the questions are chosen to be informative rather than at random.
